# Express actions

## Goal

Demonstrate forms for individual actions, related instructions, and their scope.

## Examples

### Direct instructions

An imperative names the action and its object.

Read the selected export. Count the records and report the total.

A paragraph keeps connected actions and qualifications together:

Compare the exported records with the original selection. Preserve their ordering and identify any records that were omitted or added. Do not modify the original data.

### Independent instructions

A list separates items that apply independently:

* Preserve field names.
* Retain original units.
* Identify missing values.

### Grouped instructions

Bold labels identify the subject of each instruction without requiring a separate heading.

Inspect the following properties of the export:

* **Content:** record count, field names, and values.
* **Representation:** encoding, delimiter, and line endings.
* **Compatibility:** supported readers and known limitations.

Nested items keep details within the scope of their parent:

* Inspect the header:
  * Check field names.
  * Identify duplicate columns.
* Inspect the records:
  * Check missing values.
  * Identify inconsistent field types.

### Headings and qualifications

Headings establish the scope of their content. Subheadings distinguish related subjects without repeating the shared context.

#### Header checks

Compare column names with the agreed schema.

#### Record checks

Check the values against their declared types.

**Bold** marks a meaningful distinction; *italics* qualify a statement. Strikethrough can identify superseded wording: ~~all records~~ selected records only.

A negative instruction can express a boundary directly: Do not include records outside the selected date range.
