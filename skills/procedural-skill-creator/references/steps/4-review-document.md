# Review the document

## Goal

Establish whether the instructions are sufficient, correctly scoped, and free of unnecessary guidance.

## Procedure

Review the document against the intended procedure and its expected result.

### Recognize problems

Use a comparison when a common explanation conceals an actual defect.

| Reasoning | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| The next action is obvious. | A prerequisite or decision may be missing. |
| The same rule appears everywhere, so it cannot be missed. | Repetition can obscure the rule's actual scope. |
| A shorter instruction is always better. | Compression can remove a condition that changes execution. |

Use a warning-sign list to identify problems during review:

* Steps that describe activity without an identifiable result.
* Instructions that assume a result the previous step may not produce.
* Tables that merely repeat surrounding prose.
* Examples that introduce requirements unsupported by the task.

Investigate each relevant sign. Remove duplicated instructions, but retain recurring guidance when it governs different actions or decisions.

### Verify the result

- [ ] Instructions identify the actions and their objects.
- [ ] Conditions, alternatives, and exceptions remain attached to the decisions they affect.
- [ ] Ordered actions preserve their dependencies.
- [ ] References and literal syntax are usable.
- [ ] The result preserves the original requirements and meaning.

Correct structural ambiguities without changing the established requirements. Inspect the rendered structure when rendering affects interpretation.
